Clogged drain or backup? Check these first

One slow drain is usually a small job. Water coming up in several places at once means the main line, and that needs a plumber.

Sewage backing up near outlets or appliances? Skip this page. Leave the house, then call 911 from outside.
  1. Stop running water

    Do not flush, run the dishwasher, or start the washer until you know where the clog is. More water has nowhere to go.

  2. See how many drains are affected

    One sink or one toilet points to a local clog. A toilet that bubbles when the sink drains, or water coming up in a tub or floor drain, points to the main line.

  3. Try a plunger

    For a single toilet or sink, a plunger with a good seal and a dozen firm strokes clears many clogs. Use a flange plunger for toilets.

  4. Skip the chemical cleaner

    Drain chemicals often fail on a full clog and leave caustic water sitting in the pipe, which is a hazard for you and the plumber.

  5. Check the lowest drain

    Look at the basement floor drain or lowest shower. Water or sewage there confirms a main line backup.

Stop and call a pro if

  • Sewage is coming up through a floor drain, tub, or shower.
  • More than one fixture is backing up.
  • Plunging does nothing, or the clog returns within a day.
  • You already poured in drain chemicals. Tell the plumber so they can protect themselves.

While you wait

  • Keep children and pets away from any sewage.
  • Do not run water anywhere in the house during a main line backup.
  • Wear gloves for cleanup and wash your hands well afterward.

These are general checks, not a diagnosis. If anything seems unsafe, stop and call a licensed professional.
