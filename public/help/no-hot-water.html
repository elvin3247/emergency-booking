No hot water? Check these first

A tripped breaker or a pilot light that went out causes many no-hot-water calls. These checks take a few minutes and need no tools.

Smell gas, or is a carbon monoxide alarm sounding? Skip this page. Leave the house, then call 911 or your gas utility's emergency line from outside.
  1. Check more than one faucet

    If only one faucet runs cold, the problem is at that fixture, not the water heater.

  2. Electric tank: check the breaker

    Look for a tripped breaker labeled water heater. Reset it once. If it trips again, leave it off and call.

  3. Gas tank: look at the status light or pilot

    Many gas heaters have a small window or a blinking light near the bottom. If the pilot is out, the relighting steps are printed on the tank. Only follow them if you do not smell gas.

  4. Check the temperature dial

    Someone may have turned it down or set it to vacation mode. Most homes use about 120 degrees.

  5. Think about recent use

    Back-to-back showers, laundry, and the dishwasher can empty a tank. Give it an hour and try again.

  6. Look for water around the tank

    A puddle under the heater means a leak, which is a different problem. Start a request for a leaking water heater instead.

Stop and call a pro if

  • You smell gas near the water heater. Leave and call 911 or your gas utility from outside.
  • The breaker trips a second time.
  • The pilot will not stay lit.
  • You see water, rust streaks, or scorch marks on the tank.

While you wait

  • Do not turn the temperature dial to its highest setting to make up for it.
  • If the tank is leaking, shut the cold water valve on the pipe above it.
  • Note the brand and the age on the label for the plumber.

These are general checks, not a diagnosis. If anything seems unsafe, stop and call a licensed professional.
