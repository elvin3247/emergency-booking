No cooling? Check these first

Before you pay for a visit, rule out the simple causes. These checks take about ten minutes and need no tools.

Smell gas, or is a carbon monoxide alarm sounding? Skip this page. Leave the house, then call 911 or your gas utility's emergency line from outside.
  1. Check the thermostat

    Set it to Cool and a few degrees below the room temperature. If the screen is blank or dim, replace the batteries.

  2. Check both breakers

    Central air usually has two: one for the indoor unit and one for the outdoor unit. Reset a tripped breaker once. If it trips again, leave it off.

  3. Look at the filter

    A clogged filter chokes airflow and can freeze the system. If it is gray and packed with dust, replace it.

  4. Look at the outdoor unit

    Clear leaves, grass clippings, and anything stacked against it. Check whether the fan on top is spinning when the system is on.

  5. Look for ice

    Ice on the copper lines or the indoor unit means stop. Turn cooling off, set the fan to On so it thaws, and call a contractor. Running it frozen can damage it.

  6. Check for water

    A full drain pan can trip a safety switch that shuts the system down. Water near the indoor unit points to a clogged drain line.

Stop and call a pro if

  • The breaker trips a second time.
  • You see ice on the lines or the indoor unit.
  • You smell burning or hear grinding, screeching, or loud banging.
  • The outdoor unit hums but the fan does not spin.

While you wait

  • Close blinds and curtains on sunny windows and run fans.
  • Check on older relatives, young children, and pets. They overheat fastest.
  • Confusion, fainting, or hot dry skin in the heat is an emergency. Call 911.

These are general checks, not a diagnosis. If anything seems unsafe, stop and call a licensed contractor.
