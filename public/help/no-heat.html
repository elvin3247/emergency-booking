No heat? Check these first

Plenty of no-heat calls end with a dead thermostat battery or a switch someone bumped. These checks take about ten minutes and need no tools.

Smell gas, or is a carbon monoxide alarm sounding? Skip this page. Leave the house, then call 911 or your gas utility's emergency line from outside.
  1. Check the thermostat

    Set it to Heat and a few degrees above the room temperature. If the screen is blank or dim, replace the batteries.

  2. Find the furnace switch

    Most furnaces and boilers have a power switch on or near the unit that looks like a light switch. Make sure it is on.

  3. Check the breaker

    Look for a tripped breaker labeled furnace, boiler, or heat. Reset it once. If it trips again, leave it off and call a contractor.

  4. Look at the filter

    A clogged filter can make a furnace overheat and shut itself off. If the filter is gray and packed with dust, replace it.

  5. Clear the vents

    Make sure room vents are open and not blocked. If white plastic pipes run from your furnace to the outside, check that snow, ice, or leaves are not blocking them.

  6. Check your fuel

    On oil or propane, check the tank gauge. An empty tank is a call to your fuel company, not an HVAC contractor.

  7. Heat pump in a cold snap

    Light frost on the outdoor unit is normal and clears on its own. If the unit is cased in ice or not running at all, it needs service.

Stop and call a pro if

  • You smell gas or a carbon monoxide alarm sounds. Leave the house and call 911 or your gas utility from outside.
  • The breaker trips a second time.
  • You see water around the unit, scorch marks, or smell burning.
  • The furnace starts, then shuts off again within a few minutes.

While you wait

  • Never heat your home with an oven, stove, or grill.
  • Keep space heaters three feet from anything that can burn, and turn them off when you leave the room or sleep.
  • In freezing weather, open cabinet doors under sinks and let faucets drip to protect pipes.

These are general checks, not a diagnosis. If anything seems unsafe, stop and call a licensed professional.
