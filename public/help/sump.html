Sump pump not working? Check these first

When it is raining, a stopped sump pump is urgent. Many failures are a tripped outlet or a stuck float switch, which you can check in a few minutes without tools.

Smell gas, or is a carbon monoxide alarm sounding? Skip this page. Leave the house, then call 911 or your gas utility's emergency line from outside.
  1. Stay out of standing water

    If water has reached outlets, cords, or the furnace, do not step in it. Shut off power to the basement at the panel only if you can reach it from a dry spot, or wait for a pro.

  2. Check the outlet

    Most pumps plug into an outlet with Test and Reset buttons. Press Reset. If there is no power, check the breaker panel for a tripped breaker.

  3. Check the plug

    Make sure the pump is plugged in firmly. Some pumps have two plugs stacked together, one for the switch and one for the pump.

  4. Look at the float

    The float is the part that rises with the water and turns the pump on. If it is stuck against the side of the pit or tangled in the cord, gently free it.

  5. Listen for humming

    A pump that hums but does not move water may be jammed or clogged. Unplug it before you look, and call a pro if it is stuck.

  6. Check the outside pipe

    Find where the discharge pipe ends outside. If it is frozen, buried, or blocked, the water has nowhere to go.

Stop and call a pro if

  • Water is rising and the pump will not run after you reset the outlet.
  • Water is near outlets, cords, the furnace, or the water heater.
  • The breaker trips again when the pump turns on.
  • The pump runs constantly but the water level does not drop.

While you wait

  • Move belongings, boxes, and anything electrical up off the basement floor.
  • Cut back on water use: skip laundry, the dishwasher, and long showers, since that water drains to the pit in some homes.
  • If you have a wet vac, you can lower the pit while you wait, as long as you are not standing in water.

These are general checks, not a diagnosis. If anything seems unsafe, stop and call a licensed professional.
