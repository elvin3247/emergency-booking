Loud noise or burning smell? Read this first

Some sounds and smells are harmless. Others mean shut it off now. Here is how to tell them apart.

Smell gas, or is a carbon monoxide alarm sounding? Skip this page. Leave the house, then call 911 or your gas utility's emergency line from outside.
  1. Dusty smell at first start

    A light burning-dust smell the first time the heat runs each fall is common and should fade within an hour or so.

  2. Electrical or plastic smell

    Turn the system off at the thermostat and its breaker. This can be an overheating motor or wiring, and it needs a contractor.

  3. Rotten egg smell

    That is the warning odor added to natural gas. Leave the house and call 911 or your gas utility from outside.

  4. Grinding or screeching

    Usually a failing motor or bearing. Turn the system off so it does not do more damage.

  5. Loud bang at start-up

    A single boom when a furnace lights can mean delayed ignition. Turn it off and have it checked before running it again.

  6. Rattling

    Often a loose panel or a stick in the outdoor unit. With the power off, check that panels are snug and nothing is caught in the fan.

Stop and call a pro if

  • You see smoke or flames. Get out and call 911.
  • You smell gas or a carbon monoxide alarm sounds.
  • A burning smell does not fade, or comes back.
  • Grinding, screeching, or banging happens every time it runs.

While you wait

  • Leave the system off.
  • If you turned off a breaker, leave it off until a contractor says otherwise.
  • Note when the noise or smell happens: at start-up, while running, or at shut-off.

These are general checks, not a diagnosis. If anything seems unsafe, stop and call a licensed professional.
