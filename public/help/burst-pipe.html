Burst or leaking pipe? Do this first

The first few minutes matter most. Stopping the water comes before anything else, and you can do it without tools.

Water near outlets, the electrical panel, or a sagging ceiling? Skip this page. Leave the house, then call 911 from outside.
  1. Shut off the main water valve

    It is usually where the water line enters the house: in the basement, a crawl space, or a utility closet, often near the water meter. Turn a round handle clockwise until it stops, or turn a lever a quarter turn so it sits across the pipe.

  2. Open a faucet

    Run cold water at the lowest faucet in the house to drain what is left in the pipes and take the pressure off the leak.

  3. Keep water away from electricity

    If water is near outlets, appliances, or the panel, turn off the breakers for that area, but only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.

  4. Catch and soak it up

    Put a bucket under the drip and use towels or a wet vacuum on the floor. Move rugs, boxes, and furniture out of the way.

  5. Watch the ceiling

    A bulging ceiling is holding water and can come down. Keep people out from under it and tell the plumber.

  6. Find the spot if you can

    Note where the water is coming from and whether it stopped once the main was off. That tells the plumber a lot before they arrive.

Stop and call a pro if

  • Water keeps flowing with the main valve off.
  • You cannot find or turn the main valve.
  • Water reached outlets, wiring, or the electrical panel.
  • A ceiling is sagging or dripping in more than one place.

While you wait

  • Leave the main water off until the pipe is repaired.
  • Take photos of the damage for any insurance claim.
  • In freezing weather, keep the heat on so other pipes do not freeze.

These are general checks, not a diagnosis. If anything seems unsafe, stop and call a licensed professional.
