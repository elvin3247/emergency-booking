Water around the unit? Do this first

Most water around a furnace or air handler is condensation that cannot drain. It is rarely dangerous, but it can ruin floors and ceilings quickly.

Smell gas, or is a carbon monoxide alarm sounding? Skip this page. Leave the house, then call 911 or your gas utility's emergency line from outside.
  1. Turn the system off

    Switch the thermostat to Off so the unit stops making more water.

  2. Stay safe around the water

    If water is touching wiring or the unit itself, turn off its breaker before you go near it. Do not stand in water while touching the equipment.

  3. Soak it up

    Towels or a wet vacuum now will save the floor. If the unit is in the attic, check the ceiling below for stains.

  4. Look at the filter

    A clogged filter can freeze the coil. When that ice melts, it overflows the drain pan. Replace a dirty filter.

  5. Find the drain line

    A plastic pipe carries water from the indoor unit to a drain or outdoors. If it is clogged with slime, water backs up. A contractor can clear it quickly.

  6. Note what kind of system it is

    Water under a boiler or water heater is a different problem from AC condensation. Tell the contractor which you have.

Stop and call a pro if

  • Water is coming from a boiler or its pipes.
  • Water is near wiring or the electrical panel.
  • The leak returns after you dry it and restart the system.
  • You see a stain spreading on a ceiling.

While you wait

  • Leave the system off until someone has looked at it.
  • Move rugs, boxes, and furniture away from the water.
  • Take a few photos. They help the contractor and any insurance claim.

These are general checks, not a diagnosis. If anything seems unsafe, stop and call a licensed professional.
