Before the pro arrives

What can't wait, what usually can, and what to do first. If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 or your gas utility from outside.

No hot water

Call now if: You smell gas near the water heater: leave and call 911 or your gas utility from outside. There is also water under the tank.

Usually can wait: No hot water on its own is rarely dangerous. It can usually wait for a same-day or next-day visit.

First: Check more than one faucet. Electric tank: check the breaker. Gas tank: look at the pilot or status light.

Water heater leaking

Call now if: Water is coming from the bottom of the tank. Water is spreading toward outlets, the furnace, or finished floors.

Usually can wait: A slow drip from a fitting on top of the tank can usually wait a day, with the cold valve shut and a pan under it.

First: Shut the cold water valve on the pipe into the top of the tank. Turn off the breaker (electric) or set the gas knob to Off or Pilot.

Helpful to have ready: whether the tank is gas or electric, its size in gallons, and the brand and age from the label.
