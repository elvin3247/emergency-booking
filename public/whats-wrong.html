Basement and utility room

Where the furnace or boiler, the water heater, the sump pump, and the main water valve usually live.

No heat

Furnace, boiler, or heat pump not heating.

Call a pro now if
  • It is near or below freezing outside, or will be tonight.
  • Babies, older adults, or anyone with a health condition is in the house.
  • You also smell gas or a carbon monoxide alarm is sounding: leave and call 911 from outside.

Usually can wait: In mild weather, with a safe way to stay warm, it can often wait for a daytime visit.

Do this first
  1. Check that the thermostat is set to Heat and has power.
  2. Make sure the switch on or near the furnace or boiler is on.
  3. In freezing weather, open cabinet doors under sinks and let faucets drip.

Loud noise or burning smell

Banging, grinding, or an electrical smell.

Call a pro now if
  • You smell burning or something electrical, or see smoke: turn the system off at the thermostat. If there is smoke, leave and call 911.
  • Loud banging or grinding started suddenly.

Usually can wait: A new, mild noise with no smell can usually wait for a daytime visit. Turn the system off if it gets louder.

Do this first
  1. Turn the system off at the thermostat.
  2. Note what the noise sounds like and when it happens. It helps the pro.

Sump pump not working

Water rising in the pit or basement.

Call a pro now if
  • It is raining, or the water in the pit is near the top.
  • Water is already on the basement floor.

Usually can wait: In dry weather with a low pit, it can usually wait for a daytime visit. Get it fixed before the next storm.

Do this first
  1. Do not step in water that may touch outlets or cords.
  2. Press Reset on the pump outlet and check the breaker.
  3. Free the float if it is stuck against the side of the pit.

No hot water

Water heater not heating or running out fast.

Call a pro now if
  • You smell gas near the water heater: leave and call 911 or your gas utility from outside.
  • There is also water under the tank.

Usually can wait: No hot water on its own is rarely dangerous. It can usually wait for a same-day or next-day visit.

Do this first
  1. Check more than one faucet.
  2. Electric tank: check the breaker. Gas tank: look at the pilot or status light.

Water heater leaking

Water under or around the tank.

Call a pro now if
  • Water is coming from the bottom of the tank.
  • Water is spreading toward outlets, the furnace, or finished floors.

Usually can wait: A slow drip from a fitting on top of the tank can usually wait a day, with the cold valve shut and a pan under it.

Do this first
  1. Shut the cold water valve on the pipe into the top of the tank.
  2. Turn off the breaker (electric) or set the gas knob to Off or Pilot.

Kitchen and bathrooms

Sinks, tubs, and toilets. Most problems here start at one fixture, so check whether other drains are affected.

Clogged drain or backup

Sink, tub, toilet, or sewer line backing up.

Call a pro now if
  • Sewage is coming up through a tub, shower, or floor drain.
  • Several drains in the house are backing up at once.

Usually can wait: One slow sink or tub, with other drains working, can usually wait for a daytime visit.

Do this first
  1. Stop running water and flushing until it clears.
  2. Check whether other drains are slow too. That tells the plumber where to look.

Toilet problem

Overflowing, running nonstop, or will not flush.

Call a pro now if
  • It is overflowing and will not stop, even with the valve turned off.
  • It is your only toilet and it will not flush.

Usually can wait: A toilet that runs nonstop or flushes weakly can usually wait. Turn the valve behind it off between uses.

Do this first
  1. Turn the valve behind the toilet clockwise to stop the water.
  2. Do not flush again until the bowl drains.

Walls, ceilings, and thermostat

Pipes run inside walls and ceilings, and the thermostat runs the whole heating and cooling system.

Thermostat not working

Blank screen or system will not respond.

Call a pro now if
  • The heat is out too, and it is freezing outside.

Usually can wait: A blank or unresponsive thermostat usually is not urgent if the house is a safe temperature.

Do this first
  1. Replace the batteries if it has them.
  2. Check the breaker for the furnace or air handler. Many thermostats lose power when the system does.

Burst or leaking pipe

Water spraying, dripping, or a wet ceiling or wall.

Call a pro now if
  • Water is spraying or pouring.
  • Water is coming through a ceiling, or the ceiling is sagging. Stay out from under it.
  • Water is near outlets, lights, or the electrical panel.

Usually can wait: A slow drip you can catch in a bucket can sometimes wait for the morning, if you shut the water off.

Do this first
  1. Shut the main water valve, usually where the water line enters the basement or near the water meter.
  2. Open a faucet on the lowest floor to drain the pipes.
  3. Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel from a dry spot.

Attic and outside

Many homes have the air handler in the attic and the AC or heat pump unit outside.

No cooling

AC or heat pump not cooling.

Call a pro now if
  • It is a heat wave and babies, older adults, or anyone with a health condition is in the house.
  • You smell burning or the outdoor unit is buzzing loudly but not running: turn it off.

Usually can wait: On a mild day, no cooling can usually wait for a daytime visit.

Do this first
  1. Set the thermostat to Cool and a few degrees below room temperature.
  2. Check the breakers for the AC and the indoor unit, and replace a dirty filter.
  3. If the outdoor unit is iced over, turn the system off and let it thaw.

Water leaking from unit

Water around the furnace or air handler.

Call a pro now if
  • Water is coming through a ceiling below an attic unit.
  • Water is near outlets, wiring, or the furnace burners.

Usually can wait: A small puddle near the unit, caught early, can usually wait a day with the system turned off.

Do this first
  1. Turn the system off at the thermostat. This stops most condensate overflow.
  2. Put a pan or towels down and move anything stored nearby.
